History & location

A landmark on Pretorius Street

Built in 1895 and lovingly restored, the Lindley Hotel blends old-world character with the comforts of today.

Our story

The hotel and the town

Lindley was laid out in 1875 on the banks of the Vals River and proclaimed a town in 1878. It takes its name from Daniel Lindley, an American missionary who was the first ordained minister to the Voortrekkers in Natal. The Basotho name for the town, Ntha, comes from the river.

The hotel was built in 1895, just seventeen years after the town was proclaimed, and has been restored to keep its original character. Its sandstone walls, deep veranda and lace ironwork still speak of that era.

The town itself has lived through remarkable history, including the Anglo-Boer War, when Lindley briefly served as the seat of the Free State government in May 1900. Today it's a quiet farming town with old churches, river walks and a warm country welcome.

The grounds

Gardens, courtyards & old stone

Behind the veranda you'll find rose and lavender gardens, a round stone terrace with a fire pit, old sandstone outbuildings and a thatched lapa. Inside, look up at the pressed-steel ceilings and look out for treasures like the old gramophone in the lounge.

While you're here

Things to see in and around Lindley

  • The Vals River, where a weir holds back a long stretch of water for fishing, boating and picnics
  • Lindley Roller Mills, the town's working mill
  • The Dutch Reformed Church and the town's other historic churches
  • The Lindley clock tower
  • Anglo-Boer War history, including the 1900 battle sites near town
  • A history tour through town
